# Archimedes' Principle

When an object is immersed completely or partially in a fluid at rest, it experiences an upward buoyant force. This buoyant force is equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by the object.

## The Formula

The buoyant force $F_b$ is calculated as:

$$
F_b = V \cdot \rho \cdot g
$$

Where:
- $V$ = Volume of the displaced fluid (or the submerged volume of the object) in $m^3$
- $\rho$ = Density of the fluid in $kg/m^3$
- $g$ = Acceleration due to gravity ($9.8 m/s^2$)

## Why do things float or sink?

The apparent weight of the object when submerged is its true weight minus the buoyant force.

1. **Sink:** If the object's density is greater than the fluid's density, its weight is greater than the buoyant force ($W > F_b$).
2. **Float (Suspended):** If the object's density equals the fluid's density, $W = F_b$.
3. **Float (Surface):** If the object's density is less than the fluid's density, it floats with only a fraction of its volume submerged.
